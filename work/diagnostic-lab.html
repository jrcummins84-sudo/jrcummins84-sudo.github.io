Technical instructional design

Diagnostic Lab

A product-specific troubleshooting experience designed to teach technicians how to reason from customer complaint to symptom to diagnosis.

Instructional designTechnical trainingMultimedia learning
01 · Problem

Troubleshooting requires judgment, not recall.

Technical training can easily become a catalog of facts and procedures. Diagnostic work is different: technicians need to interpret incomplete information, distinguish symptoms from diagnoses, gather evidence and decide what to test next.

Teach the thinking, not just the answer.

02 · Learning pattern
  • Start with the complaintBegin where a real service interaction begins—with the customer’s description of the problem.
  • Separate symptom from diagnosis“Battery won’t charge” is a complaint, not a diagnosis. That distinction creates room for reasoning.
  • Build product contextUse real product imagery, service scenarios, audio and technical visuals so reasoning stays connected to actual equipment.
03 · Published experience

A technical course is stronger when the learner has to interpret the situation before choosing the procedure.

04 · What this shows

Instructional design grounded in the work itself.

The project combines scenario writing, technical communication, multimedia, product context, learner navigation and collaboration with technical SMEs.
